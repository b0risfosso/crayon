Playing with satellitemap.space

Preparation
1. Choose a device and connection
   - Use a computer, tablet, or smartphone with an up-to-date web browser (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge).
   - Make sure you have a stable internet connection.

2. Optional: decide how you want the website to know your location
   - The site may ask to use your device’s location to show nearby satellite passes. Allowing this is optional. You can still use the site without sharing location.

Main actions — get started
3. Open the site
   - Go to satellitemap.space in your browser.

4. Familiarize yourself with the screen
   - Take a quick look: you should see a world map or globe with moving points (satellites). Look for a search box, a timeline/play control, and a panel or icons for settings, layers, or filters.

Exploring satellites
5. Move and zoom the map
   - Drag to pan around the Earth. Pinch or use the zoom controls (or scroll wheel) to zoom in and out. Explore different regions and watch satellites move overhead.

6. Identify a satellite
   - Click or tap on a moving dot to open its information card. Typical details include the satellite’s name, altitude, speed, and sometimes operator or mission type.

7. Search by name
   - Use the search box to find a specific satellite (for example “ISS” or another name). Select it to center and highlight it on the map.

Time controls and animation
8. Use the play/pause and time slider
   - Use the play button to see satellite motion over time, or pause to freeze the scene. A time slider often lets you jump forward or backward to see past or future positions.

9. Change animation speed
   - Look for a speed control to speed up or slow down the time simulation. This helps you watch orbits more quickly or in slow motion.

Filters and layers
10. Turn layers on or off
    - Open the layers or settings menu to show or hide elements such as orbital tracks, ground tracks, constellations, labels, or night/day shading. Try different combinations to find what you like visually.

11. Filter by type or operator
    - If available, use filters to show only certain groups (e.g., the International Space Station, communication satellites, Starlink, or active vs inactive satellites).

Following and predictions
12. Follow a satellite
    - Use a “follow” or “center” option to keep the map centered on a chosen satellite as it moves.

13. Check pass predictions
    - Look for a “pass” or prediction feature to see when a satellite will pass over your location (or a selected location). This often shows start and end times, elevation, and direction of the pass.

Customization and extra tools
14. Adjust map appearance and units
    - Explore settings to change units (kilometers/miles), map theme (dark/light), or map style. This makes the display easier to read for you.

15. Take screenshots or share views
    - Use your device’s screenshot function or a share button (if available) to save or share a particular view or satellite track.

Completion and review
16. Wrap up and reflect
    - When you’re finished, pause the animation and close the site or bookmark it for future visits. Note any satellites or passes you want to watch again.

17. Safety and privacy check
    - If you allowed location access and want to remove it later, you can revoke the site permission in your browser or device settings.

Enjoy exploring — try watching the same area at different simulated times, following a favorite satellite, or comparing how many satellites are visible over different parts of the world.
