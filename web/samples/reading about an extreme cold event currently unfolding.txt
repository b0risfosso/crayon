How to read about an extreme cold event that’s unfolding

Preparation
1. Get your device ready
   - Charge your phone or have a radio and spare batteries ready.
   - Find a quiet place where you can read without rushing.

2. Gather basic contact/info tools
   - Have a notepad or a notes app ready to jot down important facts (times, temperatures, closures).
   - Know how to contact household members or neighbors if you need to share urgent information.

Main actions: find, check, and understand reliable information
1. Start with official local sources
   - Check your local government or emergency management office and your country’s national weather service (for example, “National Weather Service,” “Met Office,” or your national meteorological agency).
   - Look for clearly labeled alerts, warnings, and safety guidance.

2. Look at the latest forecast and timing
   - Note the expected lowest temperatures, wind chill, and the start and end times of the event.
   - Pay attention to changes: forecasts can be updated frequently during extreme events.

3. Check watches, advisories, and warnings (simple meaning)
   - Advisory/watch/warning: generally, an advisory means be prepared, a watch means conditions may develop, and a warning means dangerous conditions are expected now.
   - Read any recommended actions that accompany these notices.

4. Verify impacts and local guidance
   - Look for information about road closures, public-transport changes, shelter openings, utility outage reports, and school or business closures.
   - Prefer official postings from local authorities rather than secondhand reports.

5. Cross-check with reputable media and multiple sources
   - Use well-known news outlets and the official social media accounts of emergency services or weather agencies to confirm details.
   - Check timestamps to make sure information is current.

6. Be cautious with social media and rumors
   - Treat unverified posts, photos, or videos skeptically. Confirm with an official source before acting on or sharing them.

7. Note practical safety advice you read
   - Record any concrete recommendations (stay off roads, conserve heat, check on vulnerable neighbors, boil water notices, etc.).
   - If you see evacuation or shelter instructions, note locations and how to get there.

8. Set up alerts and a monitoring plan
   - Turn on official push alerts on your phone or sign up for text/email alerts from local authorities.
   - Decide how often you will re-check (for example, every 1–3 hours during the event or when new alerts are issued).

Finish and review
1. Summarize what you learned
   - Write a brief list: expected severity and timing, immediate actions recommended, and any local impacts (closures, outages, shelters).

2. Decide on next steps for you and your household
   - If guidance suggests immediate action, follow official safety instructions. Otherwise, prepare supplies and be ready to act.

3. Keep monitoring until the event is over
   - Re-check official sources at the interval you chose and after any new alerts.
   - Share verified, important updates with family, neighbors, or others who may be affected.

4. After the event: review and follow post-event guidance
   - Look for official information about when it’s safe to travel, reports about infrastructure or utility restoration, and any health or safety advisories.

If anything you read conflicts or seems unclear, prioritize direct guidance from local emergency management or the official weather service and contact them if necessary.
