How to Build Basketball IQ

Preparation
1. Set clear goals and mindset
   - Decide what “better basketball IQ” means for you (faster reads, better positioning, smarter passes).
   - Commit to regular practice and learning. Be patient—improvement takes time.

2. Gather basic tools
   - Have a notebook or phone to take notes, a ball, and access to game videos (full games or clips).
   - Find teammates or a coach you can practice and get feedback with.

3. Assess your starting point
   - Play or watch a game and note one or two areas you want to improve (e.g., recognizing mismatches, help defense, timing of cuts).

Main Action Steps
4. Learn the basic principles of the game
   - Make sure you understand simple rules and common offensive/defensive ideas: spacing, ball movement, cutting, screening, help defense, and rebounding.
   - If anything is unclear, ask a coach or look up short explanations or videos.

5. Watch games actively, not passively
   - While watching, focus on one thing at a time: spacing, where the ball goes, how players move without the ball, or the defense’s alignment.
   - Pause and rewind to study a play. Ask: Why did that pass work? Why did the defense collapse?

6. Study position roles and common situations
   - Learn what each position is generally responsible for on offense and defense.
   - Study common situations: pick-and-roll, fast break, late-game situations, and how players create or defend advantages.

7. Practice decision-making in drills
   - Do small-sided games (1-on-1, 2-on-2, 3-on-3) to increase touches and decisions.
   - Use “read and react” drills where you must make choices based on how teammates or defenders move.
   - Simulate game scenarios (e.g., 20 seconds left, down by two) to practice decisions under pressure.

8. Improve off-ball skills and awareness
   - Work on moving without the ball: cutting, setting screens, and filling spots.
   - Practice scanning the court: every few seconds, look up to see where teammates and defenders are.

9. Learn to communicate
   - Practice calling out switches, screens, and help on defense.
   - Use clear, concise language in games. Good communication prevents mistakes and improves team IQ.

10. Watch and learn from better players
    - Study clips of players who are praised for smart play. Note their positioning, timing, and choices.
    - Compare their decisions to what you or your teammates do.

11. Review your own play with feedback
    - Record yourself playing when possible. Watch short clips and ask: What choices did I make? What could I have done differently?
    - Ask teammates or a coach for specific feedback. Act on one or two suggestions at a time.

Completion and Ongoing Review
12. Track progress and set new targets
    - Every few weeks, review your notes and videos. Mark improvements and remaining weaknesses.
    - Set a new, specific goal (for example: “Make the right read on pick-and-roll situations 8 out of 10 times in practice”).
    - Keep learning—basketball IQ grows over years through repetition, watching, and thoughtful practice.

Tips to remember
- Focus on one or two learning points at a time.
- Quality of reps matters more than quantity—practice with attention.
- Be patient and open to feedback; smart basketball comes from experience and reflection.

Follow these steps consistently, and your understanding of the game—and your on-court decisions—will improve.
